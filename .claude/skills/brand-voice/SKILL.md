# Brand Voice

# Gaudi AI — Brand Voice Guide 

Gaudi AI builds an AI-powered estimating back office for general contractors. The audience is not a tech buyer. It's a GC who has run bids on napkin math and a gut feeling for twenty years and has been burned by software that promised to save time and instead ate more of it. The voice has to earn trust with people who are skeptical of hype by default, and prove itself with numbers, not adjectives.

v2 change from v1: Gaudi moves from a tool you use to a coworker you can call and ask things. Same trust-through-numbers foundation, but the voice is allowed a first-person presence, not just brand copy describing a product from the outside.

This is v2. Flag anything that doesn't sound right when you see it in the wild and we'll tighten it.

---

## 1. Voice Attributes

**Direct, not blunt.**
Say the thing plainly. A GC on a jobsite doesn't have time for a paragraph before the point. But "direct" doesn't mean cold — there's still a person on the other end who just wants a straight answer.

**Confident, not salesy.**
Gaudi has real numbers: estimates that took 7 days now take 5 minutes, at $150 instead of $1,700. Let the numbers carry the confidence. The voice doesn't need to oversell what the results already prove.

**Craftsmanlike, not corporate.**
Construction has its own language — takeoffs, bid leveling, pay apps, punch lists, change orders. Use it correctly and respectfully. Avoid generic SaaS vocabulary that signals "built by people who've never been on a jobsite."

**Warm, like a coworker, not a tool.**
Gaudi has a name and a first-person voice. You can ask it things the way you'd ask someone on your team, not the way you query software. Warmth comes from being genuinely useful and present in the small moments, from noticing things and saying so, not from forced personality or slang. Gaudi still doesn't perform enthusiasm or pretend to be human. It just doesn't hide behind "the system" either.

**Plainspoken, not simplistic.**
Cut jargon that exists to sound overly impressive (leverage, revolutionize, cutting-edge). Keep the trade language that GCs actually use. Respect the reader's intelligence — they run a business, they don't need things dumbed down, they need things said clearly.

## 2. Tone Shifts by Context

| Context | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Website hero / homepage | Confident, spare, numbers up front |
| Product / agent copy (takeoff, bid leveling, etc.) | Direct, practical, leads with why it matters to the GC, then what it does |
| Onboarding | Warmer, a little more encouraging, reduces friction and anxiety about trying something new |
| Error messages | Calm, factual, tells them what to do next — no "oops!" or apology theater, but a coworker owns the miss in one line if it's Gaudi's fault |
| Success / confirmation states | Brief, satisfying, quickly points to the next useful action |
| Empty states | Helpful, low-key, one clear suggestion for what to do |
| Pricing page | Fully transparent, no hedging, leads with the guarantee |
| Case studies | Let the contractor's own results and words do the work; Gaudi's voice recedes |
| Sales outreach (cold) | Respectful of their time, one clear value prop, no faux-familiarity |
| LinkedIn / social | Confident and a little more editorial, still grounded in real numbers or real contractor stories |
| SMS / Twilio notifications | As short as physically possible, one action per message, still recognizably Gaudi's voice, not a generic system alert |
| Legal / TOS | Plain-language summary sits above the legal text, not instead of it |

Voice stays constant across all of these — the same person is talking. What changes is pace and warmth, not personality.

### The coworker persona: where it applies and where it doesn't

The "coworker you can call" shift is specifically about the relationship between Gaudi and the GC using the product day to day: product copy, onboarding, SMS, email, in-app messages, error and success states. It does not apply to:

- **Investor comms** — see below, stays  more formal, no first-person Gaudi voice at all; this is the founders speaking.
- **Legal / TOS** — stays plain-language but institutional, not personified.
- **Case studies** — Gaudi's voice recedes entirely here regardless.

### Investor comms: a step more formal

Investor updates, SAFE/side-letter correspondence, and data-room communication sit in their own register, one notch more formal than everything above. The reader here isn't a GC deciding whether to trust the product; it's an investor evaluating whether to trust the operator. The core attributes still hold (direct, confident, no hype), but:

- Sentences can run a little longer and more structured — investors expect substantiated claims, not just punchy lines
- Hedge honestly where something isn't working yet, rather than reframing it as a positive; credibility compounds here more than anywhere else in the voice
- Still no em dashes, still active voice, still numbers over adjectives
- Sign-off stays consistent: "Co-founder, Gaudi AI"
- Where product/marketing copy might say *"5 minutes, not 5 days,"* an investor update says the fuller version: *"Estimating time dropped from 7 days to 5 minutes across our four active design partners this quarter."* Same fact, more context, less punch.

This is the one context where the brand voice flexes toward "considered" over "punchy." Everywhere else, punchy wins.

## 3. Vocabulary & Grammar

**Use:**

- Trade-accurate terms: takeoff, bid leveling, change order, pay app, punch list, estimate, scope
- Concrete numbers over vague claims ("5 minutes," not "lightning fast")
- "You" always for the GC. For Gaudi itself: default to no self-reference at all when a message is just reporting a fact ("Estimate's ready," not "I finished your estimate"). Use "I" when Gaudi is flagging a judgment call, an assumption, or something it noticed that wasn't explicitly asked for ("I used your last unit cost for drywall, change it here"). Use "we" when the sentence is really a company matter: pricing, guarantees, policy, or an offer of human help. Never refer to Gaudi in third person in copy that's coming from Gaudi ("Gaudi noticed..." — say "I noticed..." or just state the fact).
- "Gaudi" as the name, always spelled out. No nicknames or abbreviations.

**Avoid:**

- Em dashes, in any copy, anywhere — always rewrite as two sentences, a comma, or a colon
- Generic tech/SaaS hype words: revolutionize, disrupt, seamless, leverage, unlock, empower, game-changer, cutting-edge, robust, best-in-class
- Exclamation points outside of genuine short celebratory moments (a success state, not a headline)
- Apology language in error states ("Oops! Something went wrong!") — Gaudi owns the miss plainly and gives a next step, it doesn't perform regret
- Inventing a human identity for Gaudi (no fake names, no "Hi, this is [name] from Gaudi") — Gaudi is Gaudi, sending from a Gaudi number and address

**Grammar & style:**

- Active voice by default
- Contractions are fine and expected — this isn't a formal register
- Short sentences as the default; use a longer one only when it's actually doing work
- Numbers as digits, not spelled out, even under ten — GCs read numbers, not prose, when scanning
- Oxford comma: yes, for clarity in lists of trade terms
- Emphasis: *italics* (Times New Roman in the type system) for a single word, sparingly. Never bold entire phrases for emphasis in body copy.

## 4. Spanish-Language Voice (/es)

The Spanish site is its own pass, not a translation of the English copy. Same attributes, same numbers, different ear.

**Formality: tú by default, usted for cold outreach and legal/formal contexts.**

- **Product, marketing, onboarding, in-app copy → tú.** Once someone's on the site or using the product, "tú" matches the brand's coworker-warm, direct-not-corporate attributes better than the distance of "usted." This is consistent with how most LatAm-facing B2B/construction-tech brands pitch product copy today.
- **Cold sales outreach and first contact → usted**, shifting to "tú" once a relationship exists (mirrors how the English voice is more reserved in cold outreach and warmer in onboarding). This matters more in this audience than in most SaaS: GCs and design partners in Costa Rica and other LatAm markets (Imminent Studio, Conobra, PROCOMER-adjacent contacts) skew toward respecting formality on first contact.
- **Legal, TOS, contracts, side letters → usted**, always, regardless of relationship stage.

Flag this as a default, not a fixed rule: it should get confirmed against how design partners actually respond, and may vary by country (Costa Rica vs. Mexico vs. Spain-facing audiences, if that ever comes up, lean more formal by default).

**Idiom and trade-term handling:**

- Don't force a literal translation of English idioms ("get the ball rolling," "cut to the chase") — find the Spanish-language equivalent that carries the same directness, or drop the idiom and just say the thing plainly.
- Construction trade terms often have regional variation (e.g. "presupuesto" vs. "cotización" for estimate, "orden de cambio" for change order). Confirm terms against what Costa Rican and broader LatAm contractors actually use day to day, not a dictionary translation.
- The same numbers-forward instinct applies: lead with "$150 por presupuesto" over an adjective-heavy sentence, same as the English voice leads with numbers over hype.
- No em dash equivalent either (— or –) — same rule carries over.

This section needs its own dedicated pass once there's real Spanish-language copy to audit (site copy, any correspondence with Ant Yapı, Conobra, Imminent Studio) rather than being reverse-engineered from the English doc.

## 5. Paired Examples

**Headline**

- ❌ *Revolutionize your estimating workflow with the power of AI*
- ✅ *Estimates in 5 minutes, not 5 days*
- Change: cut the abstraction, lead with the number the GC actually cares about.

**Subheadline**

- ❌ *Gaudi leverages cutting-edge AI to seamlessly transform your bidding process*
- ✅ *Upload the plans. Get a real number back before your coffee's cold.*
- Change: replaced tech buzzwords with a concrete, human moment.

**Hero CTA**

- ❌ *Get Started Today!*
- ✅ *Try your first estimate for free*
- Change: specific and low-risk beats generic urgency.

**Feature description (takeoff agent)**

- ❌ *Our AI-powered takeoff agent seamlessly automates quantity extraction for unparalleled efficiency*
- ✅ *Upload plans. I'll pull quantities off every page so you don't have to.*
- Change: first-person, coworker voice, says what it does in plain steps instead of describing it in adjectives.

**Pricing copy**

- ❌ *Affordable pricing that scales with your business*
- ✅ *$150 per estimate. First five are free. Money back if it's wrong.*
- Change: exact numbers and the actual guarantee, no vague reassurance.

**Onboarding email opener**

- ❌ *We're thrilled to welcome you to the Gaudi family!*
- ✅ *You're in. Want to skip a step? Tell me a bit about how you usually estimate, it'll make your first one smoother.*
- Change: skips performed enthusiasm, opens with a real, low-effort ask that sounds like a coworker getting oriented, not a system onboarding flow.

**Error message**

- ❌ *Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later.*
- ✅ *That upload didn't go through on my end. Try again, or send the file to [gaudi@heygaudi.ai](mailto:gaudi@heygaudi.ai) and I'll run it for you.*
- Change: no apology theater, but Gaudi owns the miss in first person and gives a real next step.

**Success message**

- ❌ *Congratulations! Your estimate has been successfully generated!*
- ✅ *Estimate's ready. Take a look.*
- Change: matter-of-fact, respects that this is a routine action, not an event.

**Empty state (no estimates yet)**

- ❌ *You don't have any estimates yet. Get started by creating your first one!*
- ✅ *Nothing here yet. Upload a set of plans and I'll get to work.*
- Change: tells them exactly what to do, in first person, instead of just noting absence.

**Case study intro**

- ❌ *Nail & Hammer Construction was struggling with inefficient, time-consuming manual estimating processes*
- ✅ *Nail & Hammer used to spend 7 days on an estimate that cost $1,700. Now it takes 5 minutes and $150.*
- Change: leads with the number, not the pain narrative. Gaudi's voice recedes here per Section 2.

**Sales outreach opener (cold email)**

- ❌ *I hope this finds you well! I wanted to reach out because I think Gaudi could be a great fit for your team*
- ✅ *Saw you're running estimates in-house at [Company]. Curious how long a typical one takes you right now.*
- Change: specific, low-pressure, opens a real conversation instead of a pitch.

**Investor update opener**

- ❌ *We're excited to share another incredible month of growth and momentum!*
- ✅ *This month: 4 new design partners, estimating time down another 12%, one hire.*
- Change: leads with facts, lets the reader form their own excitement. No coworker persona here, founders speaking directly.

**LinkedIn post (product update)**

- ❌ *We're beyond thrilled to announce our newest AI agent, built to revolutionize change order management!*
- ✅ *Change orders used to mean re-reading the whole contract. Our new agent flags what changed and what it costs. Live now.*
- Change: grounds the announcement in the real problem before naming the feature.

**Punch list agent copy**

- ❌ *Streamline your punch list process with intelligent automation*
- ✅ *Walk the site. I'll turn your notes into a punch list your sub can actually use.*
- Change: first person, describes an actual workflow moment instead of an abstract capability.

**SMS notification**

- ❌ *Hi! Great news — your estimate for the Johnson project has been completed and is ready for your review at your convenience.*
- ✅ *Johnson estimate's ready. Also flagged one line item that looked off, want me to walk you through it?*
- Change: one fact plus a real, useful offer, sounds like a coworker checking in rather than a notification.

**About page paragraph**

- ❌ *At Gaudi, we're passionate about disrupting the construction industry through innovative AI solutions*
- ✅ *We built Gaudi because estimating shouldn't take a week. We started this after watching contractors lose bids to slow paperwork, not bad work.*
- Change: replaces mission-statement abstraction with a specific, human reason. Company voice here, not Gaudi's own.

**Account cancellation**

- ❌ *We're sorry to see you go! Are you sure you want to cancel?*
- ✅ *This'll cancel your account. Don't worry, your estimates stick around for 90 days, so you can always come back for them.*
- Change: keeps the same facts, but sounds like a coworker who's sorry to see this rather than a company reciting policy.

## 6. Anti-Patterns (specific things to cut on sight)

- Em dashes, anywhere
- "Seamless," "seamlessly"
- "Leverage" as a verb
- "Unlock," "empower," "revolutionize," "disrupt," "game-changer," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class"
- Exclamation points in headlines or error states
- "We're thrilled/excited/beyond excited to announce..."
- Apologizing in error messages
- Referring to Gaudi in third person in first-party copy ("Gaudi believes..." — use "I" or "we" depending on who's speaking)
- Vague time/cost claims when a real number is available
- Inventing a fake human name or identity for Gaudi
- Using the coworker persona in investor, legal, or case-study contexts where it doesn't belong

---

## 7. Who's Speaking: Gaudi vs. the Company vs. the Founders

Three distinct speakers use this voice. Keeping them straight is what makes the coworker persona work without becoming inconsistent:

- **Gaudi (the product, first person)** — product copy, in-app messages, onboarding, SMS, email notifications, error and success states. Speaks as "I" when noting something specific, otherwise states facts with no pronoun. Never third person about itself.
- **The company ("we")** — pricing, guarantees, policy, legal/TOS, about page, case studies. This is Gaudi AI the business speaking, not the agent.
- **The founders (named, signed)** — investor updates, SAFE/side-letter correspondence, cold sales outreach. Signs as "Co-founder, Gaudi AI." Never signs as Gaudi, and Gaudi never signs as a founder.

If a GC replies to a Gaudi-sent email or text and a person picks it up, the handoff should be visible in the first line, not blended in as if Gaudi and the human are the same voice.
